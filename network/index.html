Fascicle 34 · Emily Dickinson, c. 1863

Atomized

Dickinson's variants work word by word, not poem by poem — McGann's word for it was atomization. This is that atomization drawn out: every base word and every variant across Fascicle 34, laid out as a network of who connects to whom.

A continuation of the 2022–23 team's own network-graph visualizations (see code/ and data_visualization/ in the repo), re-run against the manuscript-verified, fully-classified data that didn't exist yet when those were built. Data: fascicle34_final_classification.json.

Reading this graph

Every dot is a word that appears as a base text or a variant somewhere in Fascicle 34's documented apparatus. A line connects a base word to its variant, colored (and, since six colors alone can't stay tell-apart-able for every pair at a glance, also dashed) by what kind of relationship the taxonomy assigns it. A word that recurs across more than one poem — "Sun" appears as both a variant and, elsewhere, a base word — is drawn once and pulled toward all of its connections, the same convention the 2022 team's own word-co-occurrence scripts used.

Layout is a force simulation (repulsion between all words, attraction along each documented relationship) computed once on load, not read from any external library — consistent with the rest of this project's no-backend, plain-JavaScript tools. It has no claim to meaning beyond making clusters and outliers visible: a word pulled to the graph's edge has few or no repeated connections; a tight cluster shares vocabulary across several poems.
