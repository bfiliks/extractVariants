Eliminating the Other Meanings

The 2022-23 team's own method for aligning a base word with its variant: look both up in Noah Webster's 1844 dictionary, find the one sense each shares, and set the rest aside. "The meaning of the word power in A matches with the meaning of art in C. So we eliminate the other meanings." This is the small set of entries they're known to have actually consulted while doing that — not a general-purpose dictionary.

Three of these five (Self, Sun, World) are transcribed from dictionary-page screenshots embedded as images in the team's own 2023 STS conference slides — images this project's earlier text-only extraction of that file never captured. See webster1844_entries.json for the full dataset and sourcing notes.

Why so small

This isn't a digitized Webster's 1828/1844 — full editions already exist online (the team's own citations: webstersdictionary1828.com, edl.byu.edu). What was missing was much narrower: a structured, queryable record of the handful of entries the team is actually known to have looked up while arguing for a specific alignment — recoverable only because the underlying dictionary pages happened to survive as slide screenshots rather than as extractable text.

Self, Sun, and World carry a verified badge because they're direct transcriptions of a scanned period page. Power and Art carry an unverified badge: they're exactly as given in the paper's own prose, but their format — short bulleted senses with a modern-linguistics etymology notation ([ME < AFr < Late L.]) — doesn't match how a real 1844 page reads (compare the dense, run-on prose of the Self/Sun/World entries above). Most likely this is the team's own condensed paraphrase of their cited source rather than a verbatim quotation. Reproduced as they wrote it, flagged rather than corrected, since it's their own text.
