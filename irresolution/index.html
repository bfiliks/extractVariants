Fascicle 34 · Emily Dickinson, c. 1863

Choosing Not Choosing

In 84 places across one fascicle, Dickinson offered more than one wording for the same line. This page doesn't pick a winner. It asks, for each one: does the poem itself give you enough to choose — or is the choice, permanently, yours?

Sharon Cameron called this the whole shape of Dickinson's fascicle poetics: choosing not choosing. Not indecision — a deliberate refusal to let one reading retire the other. Tested against every documented variant in this one fascicle, that claim holds up almost exactly as strongly as she made it: 93% of the time, nothing in the poem tells you which word she meant to keep. "Would" and "Could" sit in the same sentence with equal right to be there. "Root" and "Core" both survive a reader's choice. The taxonomy this project built can classify what kind of relationship a variant has to its base word — repeating it, amplifying it, opposing it — without that classification ever forcing a verdict on which one is "correct." Openness isn't a gap in the method. It's the finding.

The 5% that do resolve are worth reading closely for exactly that reason — they're the exception that shows the rule is real, not just an artifact of not looking hard enough. In "I think to Live," the poem's own imagery of sun, noon, and midsummer makes "Night" a genuine reversal, not a peer option, for "time." Everywhere else below, both words are still standing.

What "resolved" and "open" mean here

Every pair below was classified against a two-level taxonomy: what kind of relationship the variant has to its base word (repeats it, amplifies it, opposes it, or is an addition rather than an alternative), and separately, whether the poem gives enough information to choose definitively between the two readings ("exclusive") or not ("non-exclusive"). That second axis is what this page visualizes. It comes from a larger project testing whether this taxonomy — designed in 2022, before the classification could actually be run — holds up against the complete, manuscript-verified apparatus for this fascicle. Full method and data: results_fascicle34_classification.md.

Classification was run by an AI model, blind to any answer key, reasoning from the taxonomy's own definitions and the poem's context. It is a research aid for seeing the shape of the whole fascicle at once — not a claim that any individual "open" or "resolved" call is beyond dispute. Reasonable readers, including the original team, could and did read some of these differently.
