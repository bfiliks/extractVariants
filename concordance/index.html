Dickinson Variant Concordance

1,400 base-text/variant readings across 412 poems where Emily Dickinson offered an alternate word or phrase in manuscript — searchable by poem or by word. Built from R.W. Franklin's critical apparatus, first extracted in 2022–23 by Ria De, Samantha Chipman, and Felix Oke (Loyola University Chicago, advised by Marta Werner) as part of the Dickinsonviz project, and published here for the first time.

1,143 distinct base→variant word pairs, ranked by how often that exact substitution recurs across the corpus. Most substitutions happen only once — repetition above 2–3 is genuinely rare in Dickinson's practice.

What this is

Emily Dickinson's manuscripts routinely offer more than one wording for a line — a word crossed or marked, an alternate written below or beside it. This concordance makes those alternate readings searchable across her known corpus, rather than legible only poem by poem inside the Emily Dickinson Archive itself.

The underlying extraction was built in 2022–23 as part of a Loyola University Chicago capstone project studying Dickinson's textual variants computationally (see the project repository for the fuller research, including a from-scratch classification taxonomy tested against Fascicle 34). This page is the first time the full-corpus concordance itself — as opposed to the Fascicle 34 case study — has been made public.

Data quality, checked honestly

The full 1,400-pair extraction has not been individually re-verified line by line against the manuscripts — that isn't practical at this scale. What has been done: the one fascicle (34) where every reading was independently re-verified against the Emily Dickinson Archive's live manuscript apparatus was used as a spot-check for the larger extraction.

Result: ~87% recall, one confirmed content error, in the one fascicle fully checked. Of 84 manuscript-verified readings in Fascicle 34, roughly 73 appear correctly in this extraction; the rest are missing (not wrong, just absent — consistent with gaps found elsewhere in this same 2022 scraping pass) or belong to variant types (a full-phrase alternate spanning two lines, a base-less pure addition, a second manuscript witness) that a word-level scraper wasn't built to catch. One clear error was found: a "Sun→Noon" reading appears here as "Sun→sustain," which doesn't match the manuscript at all.

Given that, treat this concordance as a genuinely useful research index — good for finding candidate readings fast — rather than a citation-grade source in itself. For anything going into a publication, the exact reading should be re-checked against the Emily Dickinson Archive directly, the same way Fascicle 34's own readings were.
